Common Cyber Threats

Understanding threats is the first step to defending against them. Here are the most prevalent cyber dangers you should know about.

Malware

Malicious software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to computer systems. Includes spyware, adware, trojans, and worms.

⚠️ Can steal personal data & credentials
🔍 Often disguised as legitimate software
🛡️ Prevention: Use antivirus & avoid unknown downloads
Severity
High

Viruses

Self-replicating programs that attach themselves to legitimate files and spread from device to device, corrupting data and degrading performance.

⚠️ Spreads through email attachments & USB drives
🔍 Can corrupt or delete important files
🛡️ Prevention: Keep software updated & scan files
Severity
High

Phishing

Fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information by disguising as trustworthy entities via emails, websites, or messages. The #1 attack vector today.

⚠️ Creates urgency to trick quick action
🔍 Uses fake domains that look legitimate
🛡️ Prevention: Verify sender & never click suspicious links
Severity
Critical

Ransomware

Encrypts your files and demands payment (ransom) for the decryption key. Can paralyze entire organizations and cause millions in damages.

⚠️ Average ransom: $200,000+ per incident
🔍 Often delivered through phishing emails
🛡️ Prevention: Regular backups & network segmentation
Severity
Critical
Stay Protected

Essential Safety Tips

Follow these best practices to protect yourself and your data from cyber threats.

01

Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Create passwords with at least 12 characters mixing uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols. Never reuse passwords across accounts.

Bad: password123 Good: K9#mP$vL2@xQ
02

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Add an extra layer of security by requiring a second verification method like SMS codes, authenticator apps, or hardware keys.

Best: Hardware key > Authenticator app > SMS
03

Use a Password Manager

Let a trusted password manager generate and store complex passwords. You only need to remember one master password.

Popular: Bitwarden, 1Password, KeePass
04

Change Compromised Passwords

Regularly check if your passwords have been leaked on sites like haveibeenpwned.com and change them immediately.

Tip: Set quarterly password review reminders
01

Check for HTTPS

Always verify a website uses HTTPS (padlock icon) before entering any personal information. HTTP sites are not encrypted.

Safe: https://bank.com Unsafe: http://bank.com
02

Avoid Public Wi-Fi for Sensitive Tasks

Public Wi-Fi networks can be monitored. Use a VPN or mobile data for banking, shopping, and accessing sensitive accounts.

Rule: No banking on café Wi-Fi!
03

Keep Browser Updated

Browser updates patch security vulnerabilities. Enable automatic updates and keep extensions to a minimum.

Tip: Review extensions monthly
04

Use Ad Blockers & Script Blockers

Malicious ads (malvertising) can infect your device without clicking. Use uBlock Origin and be cautious with pop-ups.

Recommended: uBlock Origin, Privacy Badger
01

Verify Sender Identity

Always check the full email address, not just the display name. Attackers often use addresses that look similar to legitimate ones.

Fake: support@amaz0n-security.com
02

Don't Click Unknown Links

Hover over links to see the actual URL before clicking. If an email creates urgency, it's likely a scam. Go directly to the website instead.

Rule: When in doubt, don't click — type the URL manually
03

Beware of Attachments

Never open unexpected attachments, especially .exe, .zip, or .docm files. Even PDFs can contain malicious code.

Dangerous: .exe, .scr, .zip, .docm, .js
04

Report Suspicious Emails

Most email providers have a "Report Phishing" button. Use it to help protect others and improve spam filters.

Tip: Forward phishing to reportphishing@apwg.org
01

Limit Personal Information

Don't share your birthday, address, phone number, or workplace publicly. Attackers use this for social engineering and identity theft.

Risk: "Just moved to 123 Oak St!" ❌
02

Review Privacy Settings

Regularly audit who can see your posts, photos, and personal details. Set profiles to "Friends Only" when possible.

Check: Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn privacy settings
03

Be Wary of Friend Requests

Don't accept requests from strangers. Fake profiles are used to gather information or spread malware through messages.

Red flag: New profile, few friends, no mutual connections
04

Think Before You Post

Photos can reveal location data (EXIF). Vacation posts signal an empty home. Information shared online lives forever.

Rule: Would you say this to a stranger? If not, don't post it.

Real-Life Examples

Study these real-world scam examples. Spot the red flags before they catch you off guard.

Phishing Email

👇 Hover over 🚩 red flags to learn why they're suspicious. Try clicking the link!

From: security-alert@amaz0n-support.com 🚩 Suspicious domain
To: you@email.com
Subject: ⚠️ URGENT: Your account has been compromised!

Dear Valued Customer,

We have detected unauthorized access to your account. Your account will be suspended within 24 hours unless you verify your identity immediately. 🚩 False urgency

Click the link below to secure your account:

https://amaz0n-verify-account.suspicious-site.com/login 🚩 Fake URL

If you do not verify within 24 hours, your account and all associated data will be permanently deleted. 🚩 Scare tactic

Sincerely,
Amazon Security Team 🚩 Impersonation

🔍 Red Flags Spotted:

  • Fake domain — "amaz0n" with a zero instead of "o"
  • Urgency & threats — "24 hours or account deleted"
  • Suspicious link — URL doesn't point to amazon.com
  • Generic greeting — "Dear Valued Customer" instead of your name
SMS Scam

+1 (555) 012-3456
🚩 Unknown sender

ALERT: Your bank account has been locked due to suspicious activity. Verify your identity NOW to avoid permanent closure: bit.ly/bnk-verify-2024 🚩 Shortened URL

2:34 AM 🚩 Odd timing

🔍 Red Flags Spotted:

  • Unknown number — Banks use official short codes, not random numbers
  • Shortened URL — Hides the actual malicious destination
  • Urgency — "NOW" and "permanent closure" create panic
  • Odd timing — Sent at 2:34 AM when you're less alert

  • Misspelled domain — "paypa1" with "1" instead of "l"
  • No HTTPS — Uses HTTP (no padlock), data is unencrypted
  • Not paypal.com — Legitimate PayPal only uses paypal.com
  • Credential harvesting — Designed to steal your login info

⛔ Stop! You Clicked a Phishing Link!

In real life, clicking this link could have taken you to a malware-infected website or a fake login page designed to steal your credentials.

🔍 What would have happened:

Link shown: amaz0n-verify-account.suspicious-site.com
Real domain: suspicious-site.com (NOT amazon.com!)
Risk: Credential theft, malware download

How to protect yourself:

🖱️ Hover over links before clicking to see the real URL in your browser's status bar
🔗 Check if the domain matches the real company (amazon.com, not amaz0n-support.com)
🌐 Type the URL manually in your browser instead of clicking email links
📞 When in doubt, call the company directly using their official number

📱 Don't Click SMS Links!

This shortened link (bit.ly/bnk-verify-2024) could redirect you to a phishing page or start a malware download on your phone.

🔍 Why this SMS is dangerous:

Sender: Random phone number (not your bank)
URL type: Shortened — hides real destination
Timing: 2:34 AM — sent when you're least alert

How to handle suspicious SMS:

🚫 Never click links in unexpected text messages from unknown numbers
🏦 Banks never send SMS with links asking you to verify your identity
📱 Open your bank app directly or call their official number to check
🗑️ Delete the message and report the number as spam
